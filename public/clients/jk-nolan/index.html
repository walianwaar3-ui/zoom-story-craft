The Translation
Problem

Standing between you and those extraordinary results, however, is a problem peculiar to the service business owner with genuine expertise.

Your expertise is what makes you valuable, and it's also what makes you hard to understand. The deeper your expertise, the more difficult the problem.

You've never had to bridge that gap, because referral bridged it for you. When someone is sent to you by a person they trust, that trust does the work—you don't have to make a stranger understand why you're their best choice, because someone already vouched that you are. Cold traffic comes with no such introduction. For the first time, you have to make your value clear to someone who arrives knowing nothing about you.

This is a translation problem, plainly. Left unsolved, it dooms a campaign before it can launch—if the market can't see that you have what they want, no amount of budget, no targeting hacks, and no production can make up for it.

Solving this problem is my business. And it's how I turn near-certain failure into the highest probability that when you spend your money, you make it back and then some.

The Method

I do this through a discipline I call First-Principles Advertising, which simply means that everything is built only on what's demonstrably true about your business. The method that bears this out in practice is the Authorization Method—it reveals what's true about your business and translates it for your market in language your buyers will understand. It runs in three phases.

01/

Authorization

This is the first phase, and the most intensive—it demands the most intellectual labor of anything I do, because the goal is to define the entire commercial identity of your business, top to bottom. It's a clarity few firms ever achieve in their entire lifespan.

First, I collect everything there is to know about your business and form my initial hypotheses. Then I come to you for two days in person, and we extract the rest—through careful interviews, observation, and questions no one has yet thought to ask you, and that you've never asked yourself. Then I synthesize all of it into two critical documents.

The first is the Persuasion Genome: the essence of your business, declared. The name is deliberate. A genome is the code an entire organism is built from, and this is the code your business is built from. It defines your business more completely than it has ever been defined—its character and its temperament, what you do and how you do it, the pillars of your positioning, the buyers you're for and the ones you aren't, the trajectory you're on— and a good deal more besides. Everything downstream refers back to it, derives its authority from it, and cannot contradict it. It is your constitution. And it rests on the foundational premise of First-Principles Advertising—that every business of real substance has a distinct commercial identity that must be revealed and cannot be invented.

The second document emerges from the first. Where the Genome is the permanent, enduring truth of your business, the Strategic Blueprint is the strategy for bringing that truth to market now. It studies and captures the market as it stands today—the desires of your prospects, the conditions and surfaces where we need to meet them, and the best instrument for converting them into customers. It then lays out exactly how we translate your essence into that market to answer those desires, and sets the Standard of Proof the plan will meet when it collides with the market.

That Standard is a number I write down and sign my name to before a dollar is spent. I can do this because nothing downstream is a guess. What we build is Authorized by the Blueprint, the Blueprint by the Genome—so when it reaches its intended market, everything it accomplishes there was already predetermined the moment we declared your essence.

03/

Proof

Now built, the Digital Acquisition Machine must prove itself against the Standard set forth in the Blueprint.

The Standard is a sensitivity analysis—a range of modeled scenarios, from conservative to optimal, every one of them projected against what your business needs to profit, given its costs, its baselines, and its ad spend. Wherever performance lands in that range, the result is profitable and sustainable by design. Each scenario is measured across a thirty-day period, against the metrics that determine profitability for your type of business:

For businesses with short sales cycles, where revenue is measurable inside the thirty-day period:

  • Cost per booked call
  • Revenue
  • ROAS

For businesses with long sales cycles, where deals take months to close and collected revenue is a lagging indicator, we measure:

  • Cost per booked call
  • Committed pipeline value
  • Projected ROAS

My commitment inside this thirty-day window, before there is any further discussion of ongoing management, is to prove the Machine's path to profitability. Proof is achieved when performance lands at or above the conservative scenario—the floor of the range, and still a genuinely profitable result. It is the least the Machine will do.
